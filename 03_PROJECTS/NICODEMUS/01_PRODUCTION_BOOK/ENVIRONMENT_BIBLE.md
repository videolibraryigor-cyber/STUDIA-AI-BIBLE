## Location Identity Lock (FROZEN)

### Basic Information
- **Location Name:** Nicodemus's Library (Hejkalim Shelum Nikodemus)
- **Historical Setting:** Jerusalem (Iudaea), 1st century CE
- **Type:** Private chamber; library and study of wealthy Torah teacher
- **Asset ID:** LOC_NICODEMUS_LIBRARY
- **Emotional Function:** Central stage for film's emotional arc; beginning and ending point of memories
- **Canonical Source:** Environment Bible v1.0 (in source ZIP)

### Historical Context

**Owner:** Wealthy Jewish teacher and council member (Sanhedrin member); educated, well-regarded

**Period:** 1st century CE (30-33 CE approximate); Second Temple period

**Geography:** Jerusalem's upper city; likely attached to residence of affluent teacher

**Accessibility:** Private chamber; not a public space; intimate setting for study and contemplation

## Architectural Definition (FROZEN)

### Walls
- **Material:** Locally quarried limestone
- **Color:** Pale cream to warm white-grey (natural limestone hue)
- **Finish:** Dressed ashlar blocks; fitted precisely but not perfectly smooth
- **Texture:** Natural stone finish; slight roughness; weathered over years
- **Age Evidence:** Discolored patches (water stains, dust shadows); patina of age
- **Dimensions:** Thick (18-24 inches); provides insulation and permanence

**Cannot Change:**
- Material (limestone; no plaster, no modern wall finish)
- Color (pale earth tone; no bright white, no modern paint)
- Surface (ashlar; no modern drywall or brick)

### Ceiling
- **Material:** Cedar wood beams
- **Color:** Dark reddish-brown (natural cedar aging)
- **Structure:** Visible beam layout; beams spaced evenly
- **Height:** Low, intimate (approximately 8-10 feet above floor; not cathedral-like)
- **Detail:** Beams show age; possibly slight sag (structure integrity not compromised; just naturalistic aging)
- **Lighting Effect:** Dark ceiling creates intimate feeling; focuses light downward

**Cannot Change:**
- Material (cedar; no modern wood, no metal)
- Color (dark; no light-colored beams)
- Height (low, not open/airy; not grand)

### Floor
- **Material:** Locally quarried stone (limestone or similar)
- **Color:** Worn grey to pale brown (unfinished natural stone)
- **Finish:** Worn, scuffed; feet of many generations walking same paths
- **Pattern:** Irregular stone blocks; fitted without mortar or with minimal mortar
- **Condition:** Naturally aged; dust catches in cracks; no pristine appearance
- **Sound Quality:** Stone produces echoes; sound carries intimately in chamber

**Cannot Change:**
- Material (stone; no tile, no modern floor)
- Finish (worn; no polished, no sealed surface)
- Pattern (natural irregular blocks; no geometric patterns)

### Windows
- **Number:** One (primary; high on wall)
- **Type:** Open (no glass; period-appropriate; no shutters unless story-driven)
- **Size:** Narrow, tall (perhaps 2-3 feet wide, 4-5 feet tall)
- **Position:** High on wall (7-8 feet up); provides natural light without creating security vulnerability
- **Light Quality:** Directional, distinct; creates distinct light/shadow line across room
- **External View:** Implies exterior; suggests time of day through light angle and quality

**Cannot Change:**
- Number (one window; not multiple)
- Type (open; no glass or shutters)
- Size (narrow, tall; not large modern window)
- Position (high; not at eye level)

### Doors
- **Primary Entrance:** Solid wooden door
- **Material:** Thick wood (cedar or acacia)
- **Fastening:** Simple wooden latch or iron lock (period-appropriate)
- **Detail:** Worn; patina of frequent use
- **Sound:** Creaks when opened (historical; no silent modern hinges)

## Furnishings & Objects (FROZEN)

### Desk (Primary Object)

**Function:** Center of chamber; where Nicodemus works and thinks; emotional focal point

**Description:**
- **Material:** Cedar wood (expensive; indicates wealth and respect for Torah)
- **Construction:** Solid; built to last centuries
- **Size:** Approximately 3-4 feet wide, 2-3 feet deep, waist-height (for writing while standing or sitting on stool)
- **Surface:** Worn smooth from years of writing; possibly ink stains (marks of use)
- **Legs:** Four sturdy legs; simple carved decoration (if any) but not ornate
- **Age:** Well-used; shows patina of age

**Position:** Center of chamber (or slightly off-center); focal point of room

**What Sits on Desk:**
- Scrolls (multiple; stacked or rolled)
- Writing implements: quills, inkwell (small clay or bronze vessel), stylus
- Stone weight or papyrus press (paperweight; keeps scrolls from unrolling)
- Possibly candle holder (moved to desk when working by dim light)

**Continuity Lock:** Once first approved shot establishes desk position, prop arrangement is locked; cannot move or rearrange without scene justification

### Shelving

**Structure:**
- **Material:** Simple wooden shelves (cedar or similar)
- **Construction:** Functional, not decorative; holds weight without flourish
- **Arrangement:** Built into wall or freestanding; 3-4 shelf levels

**Contents:**
- Multiple scrolls (standing upright or rolled; organized by subject/importance)
- Clay vessels (storage for pens, measurements, or archival purposes)
- Possibly clay lamps (stored; not currently lit)
- Books not present (codex form didn't exist in 1st century; only scrolls)

**Condition:** Dusty; scrolls somewhat disorganized (real study, not pristine library)

### Seating

**Furniture:**
- **Primary:** Wooden bench or simple chair; functional, not comfortable
- **Material:** Sturdy wood; worn smooth from use
- **Design:** Simple, no upholstery; possibly with back support or backless
- **Height:** Appropriate for reading at desk

**Condition:** Worn; age patina; scratches from movement

### Lighting Equipment (Moveable)

**Oil Lamp (Bronze, Primary Light Source)**
- **Material:** Bronze or brass
- **Size:** Hand-held or small tabletop size
- **Design:** Traditional Roman/Jewish oil lamp design (open oil well, wick)
- **Fuel:** Olive oil
- **Light Quality:** Warm, limited radius; flickers with breeze

**Candles (Tallow or Beeswax)**
- **Material:** Tallow or beeswax
- **Color:** Yellow or off-white (natural color)
- **Quantity:** One or two, on desk or shelf
- **Light Quality:** Warm, flickering; emotional marker

**Position Flexibility:** Lamp and candles move as needed for work/prayer/contemplation, but position is locked once scene establishes initial placement

**Window Light (Natural, Free Light Source)**
- **Daytime Scenes:** Sunlight through window provides primary light
- **Direction:** Specific angle based on time of day and season
- **Quality:** Cool, directional; creates hard shadows
- **Continuation:** Light direction consistent with narrative time progression

### Decorative Elements (Minimal)

**What IS Present:**
- Natural stone textures and aging
- Wood grain and patina
- Dust and light particles in air (naturalistic)

**What IS NOT Present:**
- Ornate carvings or decoration
- Colorful wall hangings or tapestries
- Decorative pottery or objects
- Luxury items (gold, silver, precious stones)
- Modern objects or materials

**Philosophy:** Room is space of scholarship and contemplation, not wealth display

## Surface Properties & Atmosphere (FROZEN)

### Dust & Light Particles
- **Visible Dust:** Historical accuracy; dust visible in light rays
- **Dust Accumulation:** On shelves, on high surfaces; indicates natural passage of time
- **In Air:** Dust particles visible when light streams through window; creates atmospheric effect
- **Function:** Shows age, naturalism, historical authenticity

### Shadows & Lighting Logic

**Natural Light (Daytime):**
- Sunlight through window creates distinct light/shadow boundary
- Shadow hard-edged, moving throughout day as sun angle changes
- Strong directional effect; limited secondary light

**Artificial Light (Evening/Night):**
- Oil lamp creates warm, limited radius of light
- Far side of room falls into shadow
- Candle provides supplementary warm light; flickers

**Combined Light (Transition Hours):**
- Both natural and artificial present (complex, realistic lighting)
- Natural light dominates; artificial enhances or supplements

**What Light Never Does:**
- Appear from no source (no unmotivated studio lighting)
- Shift unexpectedly (changes motivated by action: moving lamp, opening/closing window)
- Create modern three-point rig appearance (all light sourced from world objects)

### Atmosphere & Humidity

**Climate:** Dry Mediterranean (limestone region; minimal moisture)

**Humidity:** Dry; scrolls preserved well; no mold or rot

**Air Quality:** Clear but dusty; natural aging aromas (stone, wood, old paper)

**Sound:** Stone and wood carry sound; modest echo; intimate acoustics

## Historical Accuracy Standards (FROZEN)

### Material Authenticity

**Allowed Materials (1st Century CE Iudaea):**
- Limestone, marble, adobe brick
- Cedar, acacia, olive, pine wood
- Linen, wool, leather
- Bronze, iron, copper
- Clay, pottery
- Natural dyes (indigo, ochre, madder)
- Olive oil
- Natural plant fibers (papyrus, reeds)

**Forbidden Materials (Anachronisms):**
- Glass windows (rare and expensive in 1st century; if used, must be explicit and extraordinary)
- Concrete (Roman but not in Jerusalem residential)
- Plaster (possible but not primary)
- Plastic, vinyl, synthetic fibers
- Modern paint, stain, or finishes
- Metal hardware (modern hinges, handles, locks)

### Design Language

**Allowed:**
- Simple, functional design
- Natural materials in natural state
- Worn, aged appearance
- Modest decoration (if any)
- Honest construction (visible joinery, no hiding structure)

**Forbidden:**
- Ornate carving or embellishment
- Gothic, medieval, or Victorian design language
- Modern minimalism or contemporary design
- Hollywood "Biblical" aesthetic (gold, dramatic color, theatrical design)
- Anything suggesting wealth or luxury beyond educated teacher's means

## Continuity Rules (MUST RESPECT)

### Shot-to-Shot Consistency

**Props in Space:**
- Desk position locked after first approved shot
- Lamp position on desk locked after establishment
- Scroll positions on desk locked after establishment
- Shelving arrangement locked after establishment

**Light Sources:**
- If lamp is lit in Shot 1, must remain lit in Shot 2 (unless blown out or moved deliberately)
- Window direction consistent (light from same angle throughout sequence)
- Shadow positions match light source direction

**Environmental State:**
- Dust level consistent (same dust covering unless disturbed in scene)
- Scroll disorder consistent (organized same way unless character moves them)
- Wear patterns consistent (same scuffs on floor, same stains on desk)

### Scene Progression Flexibility

**Changes Only If Motivated:**
- Lamp moved: character must move it
- Scrolls rearranged: character must interact with them
- Window opened/closed: character must perform action
- Light source changed: time progression or character action must justify

**No Mysterious Changes:**
- Props cannot disappear between shots
- Light cannot shift without motivation
- Furnishings cannot rearrange without cause
- Surface condition cannot change without action

## Camera Constraints (Lighting for Visual Production)

### Light-Based Framing

**Available Light Directions:**
- From window (daylight): cool, directional, hard shadows
- From desk lamp: warm, limited radius, flickers
- From candle: warm, intimate, flickering
- From combination: complex, realistic (preferred)

**Forbidden Lighting Setups:**
- Three-point studio rig (no unmotivated fill lights)
- Back-lighting from non-existent source (window is only strong back-light)
- Keylight from impossible angle (shadows must match source)
- Smooth, even lighting (world lighting is uneven by nature)

### Environment Framing Options

**Establishing Shots:**
- Wide shot of full room: shows desk, shelves, door, window relationship
- Emphasizes scale (intimate, not grand)
- Helps viewer understand spatial layout

**Medium Shots:**
- Desk-focused: Nicodemus at work
- Shelf-focused: Nicodemus searching scrolls
- Near door: entrances/exits
- Near window: contemplation scenes

**Close-Ups:**
- Hands on scroll: work detail
- Face by candlelight: emotional intensity
- Hands holding quill: writing/thinking
- Eyes looking at scroll: reading/studying

**Pan/Dolly Suggestions:**
- Slow dolly around desk: exploration
- Pan across shelves: searching for knowledge
- Move toward window: seeking light/clarity
- Move toward door: contemplating departure

## Special Notes

### Emotional Function of Space

This room is not merely a setting; it is a character itself. The library:
- Represents Nicodemus's mental space
- Is stable anchor point for the entire film
- Contains the physical objects that trigger memories
- Witnesses Nicodemus's internal transformation

Camera placement and light treatment should always serve the emotional purpose of the scene, not the aesthetic.

### Historical Accuracy vs. Spectacle

The library should feel authentic and lived-in, not like a museum display or Hollywood set. Wear, dust, aging, and humble materials are not flaws; they are proof of authenticity.

### When Environment Description Conflicts with Other Documents

- Refer to source archive originals (ZIP) for tiebreaker
- Escalate ambiguous conflicts to Director
- Always privilege historical accuracy and source authority
