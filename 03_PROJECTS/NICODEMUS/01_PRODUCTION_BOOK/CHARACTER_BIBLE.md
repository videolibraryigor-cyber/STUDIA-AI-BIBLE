## Identity Lock (FROZEN)

### Basic Information
- **Character Name:** Nicodemus (Νικόδημος)
- **Historical Reference:** 1st century Jewish teacher, mentioned in Gospel of John
- **Setting:** Iudaea, Jerusalem, 30-33 CE
- **Asset ID:** CHAR_NICODEMUS
- **Canonical References:** Master Reference v1.0, Face Reference v1.0, Turnaround v1.0 (all in source ZIP)

### Age & Physiology

**Age:** 60-62 years old  
**Context:** Established Torah scholar and council member; not young; life experience etched in face and bearing.

**Appearance:**
- **Skin Tone:** Olive Mediterranean (native to Levantine region)
- **Eye Color:** Dark brown (not blue, not light)
- **Eye Character:** Attentive, intelligent, searching
- **Facial Hair:** Grey beard of medium length (not long, not trimmed close); indicates age and wisdom
- **Head Hair:** Grey, shoulder-length (traditional Jewish teaching style of period); not styled
- **Eyebrows:** Thick, expressive, often furrowed in thought
- **Nose:** Straight, prominent (not small, not broken)
- **Forehead:** Wide; suggests intellectual bearing
- **Mouth:** Thin-lipped, capable of both severity and gentleness; rarely smiles widely

**Build:**
- **Height:** Average to slightly tall (not short, not imposing)
- **Weight:** Lean, scholar's build (not muscular, not fat); indicates lifetime of study rather than labor
- **Posture:** Upright, dignified, careful with movement (scholar's awareness of presence)
- **Hands:** Careful, precise movements; hands used for gesturing during teaching; fingers long enough for writing with stylus; no calluses from manual labor
- **Gait:** Measured, unhurried; walks as if thinking between steps

**Face Structure:**
- Canonical Master Reference must be matched exactly in all shots
- Age appropriate (60-62, not younger)
- Identity must remain consistent across all shots (CRITICAL for QC)

**What Cannot Change:**
- Age (must appear 60-62)
- Hair color (grey, not brown or black)
- Eye color (dark brown, not blue)
- Ethnicity (Levantine Jewish, not European or Middle Eastern)
- Beard state (must always appear; cannot be clean-shaven)

## Performance Lock (FROZEN)

### Demeanor & Bearing

**Overall Character:** Reserved, intelligent, contemplative, slow to judge, capable of profound interior change

**Voice & Speech Pattern (if applicable):**
- Measured, thoughtful pauses between thoughts
- Not rushed; never colloquial
- Educated Aramaic/Hebrew accent and phrasing
- Occasionally uncertain (appropriate to journey of faith)

### Emotional Expression

**Primary Channel: Eyes**
- Emotion shown through gaze: focus, softening, brightening, widening
- Eyes communicate more than words
- NOT theatrical; subtle and true

**Secondary Channels:**
- Breath (visible, as indicator of emotion — fear, relief, wonder)
- Micro-movements of face (not grimacing or exaggerated)
- Posture shifts (opening chest when accepting, closing inward when struggling)
- Hand gestures (scholar's hands — precise, careful; never casual)
- Pauses and silence (full emotional beats in quiet moments)

**What Emotional Expression Is NOT:**
- Theatrical sighing or dramatic hand-wringing
- Wide-eyed shock or obvious fear
- Smiling broadly or laughing
- Angry outbursts or violent reactions
- Overstated body language or pose-based emotion
- "Movie star" acting; must feel true to era and character

### Performance Arc (Dramatic Route Reference)

See [PROJECT_BIBLE.md](PROJECT_BIBLE.md) for overall dramatic route: Curiosity → Astonishment → Conflict → Fear → Loss → Faith → Peace

**Nicodemus's Journey:**
1. **Curiosity (Early):** Eyebrows raised slightly; leaning forward; searching for meaning
2. **Astonishment (Encounter):** Eyes widen; breath catches; unexpected truth revealed
3. **Conflict (Resistance):** Jaw tightens; gaze darkens; internal struggle visible
4. **Fear (Stakes):** Eyes dart nervously; hands clasp; breathing shallow
5. **Loss (Grief):** Eyes lower; shoulders drop; moment of despair
6. **Faith (Acceptance):** Eyes clear; posture opens; breath deepens; peace in face
7. **Peace (Resolution):** Steady gaze; gentle expression; comfortable with unknowable

**Director's Note:** Performance should feel like watching thought happen, not watching acting.

## Costume Lock (FROZEN)

### Primary Outfit (Always)

**Tunic (Innermost):**
- Material: Linen (not wool, not cotton, not modern fabric)
- Color: Cream or off-white
- Cut: Simple, loose-fitting; indicative of modest means and scholar's indifference to display
- Fit: Reaches below knees; sleeves to elbow or wrist
- Detail: Minimal ornament; possibly small blue stripe (tzitzit reference) on hem
- Condition: Clean or dusty, depending on scene action

**Cloak (Outer Garment):**
- Material: Wool (not linen, not silk)
- Color: Dark — probably deep blue-grey, dark purple, or brown (period-appropriate dyes)
- Cut: Rectangular, draped; typical of 1st century Iudaean dress
- Fit: Drapes from shoulders; can be drawn up to cover head if needed
- Fastening: Simple knot or clasp (no buttons, zippers, or modern hardware)
- Condition: Worn; faded; evidence of age and use

**Belt:**
- Material: Woven textile (linen or wool)
- Color: Coordinating neutral (beige, grey, brown)
- Function: Cinches waist; holds tunic in place; practical, not decorative
- Detail: Simple weave; possible fringe on ends

**Footwear:**
- Sandals: Simple leather
- Type: Open-toed or strappy; leather straps on foot
- Condition: Worn, scuffed; feet of elderly scholar
- No socks, no modern footwear

**Jewelry (Minimal):**
- Ring: Single signet ring (identification seal)
- Material: Bronze or simple iron
- Worn on: Ring finger (tradition for teacher/authority)
- Only jewelry visible; no bracelets, necklaces, or ornament
- Worn from years of use

### Costume Continuity

**Dust & Wear:**
- Scene determines state: clean after indoor activity, dusty after outdoor travel
- Wear patterns consistent with age (faded folds, soft fabric)
- Tears or stains only if motivated by scene action
- Once established, continuity locked (props tracker: see HERO_PROPS.md)

**Costume Changes:**
- Only change if scene progression requires (travel, multiple days, emotional state change)
- All changes must be discussed and approved in Scene Package
- Changes must remain period-appropriate

**What Cannot Change:**
- Material (linen/wool; no modern synthetics)
- Color palette (creams, dark earth tones; no bright colors)
- Style (simple, modest; no fashion, no modern design language)
- Jewelry excess (ring only; no other ornament)
- Footwear style (sandals; no shoes)

## Hands & Gestures Lock

### Hand Appearance
- Hands of elderly scholar: not rough/calloused, not delicate/weak
- Skin tone matches face (consistent hue)
- Age spots or lines acceptable; indicate 60+ years
- Fingers long enough for writing/teaching (not stubby)
- Nails neat but not manicured; natural state
- No wedding ring (unless historically specified)

### Gesture Repertoire
- Hands rarely at rest in frame
- Teaching gesture: open hand (explaining)
- Searching gesture: hands together, fingers steepled (thinking)
- Protective gesture: hand over heart
- Reaching gesture: careful extension toward truth/revelation
- Withdrawal gesture: hands folding inward (doubt, grief)
- Acceptance gesture: palms open upward (surrender to truth)

**What NOT to Do:**
- Theatrical hand flourishes
- Nervous fidgeting or hand-wringing
- Grandiose gesturing
- Casual modern hand positions (pockets, resting on hip)
- Unnecessary movement; every gesture has meaning

### Hand Visibility in Shots
- Hands visible in close-ups when significant to emotion
- Hands visible when gesture carries story meaning
- Hands can be hidden only when not relevant to shot
- Hand anatomy must be correct (no distortion, no extra fingers)

## Continuity Rules (MUST RESPECT)

### Shot-to-Shot Consistency

**Between Shots in Same Scene:**
- Costume state consistent (if clean in Shot 1, must be clean in Shot 2 unless scripted change)
- Beard state consistent (always present; never suddenly clean-shaven)
- Hair length/color consistent (locked after first approved shot)
- Hand position for cutaways must match (no jump cuts in hand position)
- Emotional throughline must be coherent (cannot jump from peace to anger without motivation)

**Between Scenes (Emotional Arc):**
- Track emotional journey across full film (see Performance Arc above)
- Internal consistency required: if Nicodemus struggles in Scene 2, he cannot regress in Scene 3 without scene-specific motivation
- Performance must feel like continuous character arc, not disconnected vignettes

### Prop Interaction
- Scrolls/quills held consistently (right hand vs. left; position on table)
- Props not appearing/disappearing between shots without explanation
- Continuity of object handling (if writing with quill, hand position must match on resumption)

### Environmental Consistency
- If in Library, familiar environment; body language shows ease
- If in unfamiliar location, body language shows wariness
- Light consistency: how shadows fall on Nicodemus must match between shots

## Character Constraints for Prompt Engineers

**When Writing Image Prompts:**

1. **Always reference asset ID:** `CHAR_NICODEMUS`
2. **Never describe age numerically in main prompt** (rely on visual reference); reference "experienced Torah scholar"
3. **Always specify costume state:** "clean linen tunic," "dusty cloak," "dust-covered from travel"
4. **Always specify emotional state:** "contemplative," "searching," "grieving," "at peace"
5. **Always specify hand position if relevant:** "hands steepled in thought," "hands reaching toward scroll," "palms open"
6. **Never use terms incompatible with character:**
   - NO: "young Nicodemus" (he is 60-62)
   - NO: "aggressive Nicodemus" (he is reserved and thoughtful)
   - NO: "glamorous Nicodemus" (he is modest scholar)
   - NO: "blue-eyed Nicodemus" (eyes are dark brown)
   - NO: "clean-shaven Nicodemus" (always bearded)

**In Negative Prompt:**
- Include: "different age, different hair, different beard, different facial structure"
- Include: "blue eyes, fashion styling, modern clothing, jewelry excess"
- Include: "theatrical expression, aggressive pose, smile, modern actor"

## Special Notes

### Historical Authenticity
Nicodemus was a real historical figure mentioned in Gospel of John as a Pharisee and member of Sanhedrin (Jewish council). This character portrayal aims to be:
- Historically plausible (costume, setting, context of 1st century Iudaea)
- Scripturally informed (behavior consistent with textual hints: curious, cautious, eventually faithful)
- Humanly authentic (not mythologized; shown as man of conscience struggling with faith)

### Performance Direction Philosophy
Do not act Nicodemus; let Nicodemus think. The character's interior journey should be so clear that external performance appears effortless. Emotion through subtlety, not through theatrical display.

### When Character Description Conflicts with Other Documents
- Refer to source archive originals (ZIP) for tiebreaker
- Escalate ambiguous conflicts to Director
- Always privilege visual reference over text description
