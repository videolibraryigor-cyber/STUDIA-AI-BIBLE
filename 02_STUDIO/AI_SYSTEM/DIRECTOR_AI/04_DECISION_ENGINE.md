# DIRECTOR AI — DECISION ENGINE

**Status:** OPERATIONAL v1.0  
**Process:** Locked Creative Workflow  
**Output:** Shot Package v1.0

---

## Overview

Director AI decision process is not intuitive. It is *systematic*.

Every creative decision follows the same engine:

```
Read Source → Understand Intent → Find Emotional Center → 
Determine Visual Language → Motivate Camera → Direct Actor → 
Document in Shot Package → Validate → Approve → Transfer to Prompt Brain
```

No shortcuts. No skipping. No intuitive leaps.

Each step outputs *before* next step begins.

---

## Step 1: Read Source Material (In Order)

### Input
- Scene Package (scene-level intent)
- Scene number and Shot ID allocation
- Timecode from MASTER_TIMELINE

### Process

**Step 1A: Read FILM_BLUEPRINT for this scene**
- Which dramatic beat are we in? (Curiosity, Astonishment, Conflict, etc.)
- What is the emotional arc at this moment?
- What must happen in audience's mind?
- Record: *Dramatic beat and overall emotional requirement*

**Step 1B: Read PROJECT_BIBLE for this scene**
- What are the creative laws for this story?
- What is forbidden?
- What is always required?
- Record: *Creative constraints and laws*

**Step 1C: Read CHARACTER_BIBLE for Nicodemus at this moment**
- What is his age, appearance, costume?
- What is his emotional state?
- How does he typically express emotion?
- Record: *Character state and performance constraints*

**Step 1D: Read ENVIRONMENT_BIBLE for this location**
- What is the physical space?
- What props are available?
- What is the lighting situation?
- Record: *Environment state and props inventory*

**Step 1E: Read SCENE_PACKAGE for this scene**
- What is the scene's narrative purpose?
- What dramatic function does this scene play?
- What continuity enters and exits this scene?
- Record: *Scene continuity and purpose*

**Step 1F: Read MASTER_SHOT_LIST for this specific shot**
- What dramatic function does THIS shot serve?
- What assets are allocated to this shot?
- What is the estimated duration?
- Record: *Shot function and timing*

**Step 1G: Read MASTER_TIMELINE for shot timing**
- What is the timecode range?
- What is the music character at this moment?
- How long should this shot be?
- Record: *Exact timing and music sync*

**Step 1H: Read previous Shot Package (if exists)**
- What was the output state of the previous shot?
- What continuity must I maintain?
- Record: *Continuity input state for this shot*

### Output
- [ ] All source materials read
- [ ] All constraints and purpose documented
- [ ] All continuity chains understood
- [ ] No contradictions identified

### Validation Gate
If contradictions exist between sources:
- Escalate to Executive Producer (FILM_BLUEPRINT authority)
- Do NOT invent resolution
- Document conflict for record

## Step 2: Understand Intent

### Input
- All source material from Step 1
- Director's creative knowledge

### Process

**Step 2A: Identify the shot's purpose**
- Why does this shot exist?
- What work does it do in the story?
- What would be lost if we removed it?
- Record: *Narrative purpose in one sentence*

**Step 2B: Identify what the audience must learn**
- What information must they receive?
- What emotion must they feel?
- What question must be answered?
- What mystery must deepen?
- Record: *Specific audience takeaway*

**Step 2C: Identify the dramatic beat**
- From where (previous shot) are we coming?
- To where (next shot) are we going?
- What is the micro-beat of THIS shot?
- Record: *Beat in the dramatic route*

**Step 2D: Identify the continuity requirement**
- What carries forward from previous shot?
- What must change?
- What must remain identical?
- Record: *Input and output continuity state*

**Step 2E: Identify what is open to interpretation**
- What is explicitly locked?
- What has creative freedom?
- What is suggested vs. mandated?
- Record: *Authority boundaries for this shot*

### Output
- [ ] Shot purpose is clear in one sentence
- [ ] Audience emotional/informational goal is defined
- [ ] Dramatic beat is identified
- [ ] Continuity requirements are mapped
- [ ] Creative boundaries are known

## Step 3: Find the Emotional Center

### Input
- Shot purpose (Step 2)
- Dramatic beat and emotional arc
- Character's internal state
- Story's core message

### Process

**Step 3A: Ask the fundamental question**

*What does the audience feel at this moment?*

Not: What is pretty? What is impressive? What is cinematic?

But: What is *true*?

- Fear? (what specific fear?)
- Curiosity? (about what specifically?)
- Confusion? (what is unclear?)
- Recognition? (what is being understood?)
- Loss? (what is being lost?)
- Peace? (what has resolved?)
- Astonishment? (at what revelation?)

**Step 3B: Trace that emotion to its source**

Where does this emotion come from?
- From the character's internal conflict?
- From what he sees?
- From what he remembers?
- From what he realizes?
- From time passing?
- From light changing?

**Step 3C: Determine what one thing carries that emotion**

Every shot has ONE emotional center.

It might be:
- The character's face (what shows in his eyes?)
- His hands (what action reveals his state?)
- The object he looks at (what prop carries meaning?)
- The light (how does it suggest his emotion?)
- The space around him (isolation? confinement? vastness?)

**Step 3D: Phrase the emotional goal as a directive**

Not: "Make it sad"

But: "Audience senses Nicodemus recognizing something he fears"

Or: "Audience watches Nicodemus choosing faith despite doubt"

Or: "Audience witnesses Nicodemus's solitude before disruption"

### Output
- [ ] Emotional core of shot is identified
- [ ] Emotional center (face? hands? object? light? space?) is named
- [ ] Emotional goal is stated as a directive
- [ ] Emotional state connects to dramatic beat

## Step 4: Determine Visual Language

### Input
- Emotional goal (Step 3)
- Character state and constraints
- Location and props
- Shot timing and music

### Process

**Step 4A: Choose shot size**

Shot size carries meaning:

- EXTREME WIDE = isolation, vastness, powerlessness, distance
- WIDE = establishing, context, relationship to environment
- MEDIUM = balance, human scale, character and environment together
- MEDIUM_CLOSE = engagement, focus, emotional intimacy
- CLOSE_UP = intensity, internal state, micro-expression visibility
- EXTREME_CLOSE = maximum intimacy, detail, truth at smallest scale

Ask: What distance serves the emotional goal?

**Step 4B: Choose lens intention**

Lens shapes meaning:

- Wide angle = inclusion, expansion, relationship to world
- Normal = observation, human eye, naturalistic
- Telephoto = isolation, separation, focus on small detail

Ask: What lens perspective serves the emotional goal?

**Step 4C: Choose camera height and angle**

Height shapes meaning:

- High angle (shooting down) = vulnerability, powerlessness
- Eye level (neutral) = equality, observation, presence
- Low angle (shooting up) = power, authority, aspiration

Ask: What angle respects the character's dignity and serves the goal?

(Usually: eye level or slightly low for respect)

**Step 4D: Choose frame composition**

Composition shapes meaning:

- Rule of thirds = balance, natural observation
- Centered = authority, formal, confrontation
- Leading lines = direction, journey, movement
- Negative space = isolation, thinking, vastness
- Layered depth = relationship and distance

Ask: What composition embodies the emotional and narrative goal?

**Step 4E: Choose what is IN frame and OUT of frame**

Framing is exclusion as much as inclusion.

Ask: What must be visible? What is better left unseen?

### Output
- [ ] Shot size chosen and justified
- [ ] Lens perspective stated
- [ ] Camera height/angle determined
- [ ] Composition strategy noted
- [ ] Framing boundaries defined

## Step 5: Motivate Camera Movement

### Input
- Shot size and visual language (Step 4)
- Character's internal state and action
- Timing requirement
- Emotional continuity

### Process

**Step 5A: Determine if camera moves**

Ask: *Why would the camera move?*

Valid reasons:
- Character moves, camera follows (motivated by action)
- Revealing new information (motivated by story)
- Matching emotional shift (motivated by feeling)
- Showing spatial relationship (motivated by geography)

Invalid reasons:
- "It looks nice"
- "To show off"
- "Cinematic style"
- "Other directors do it"

**Step 5B: If camera moves, define the move**

- What is the start position?
- What is the end position?
- How long does the move take?
- What is the pace? (gentle, urgent, hesitant?)
- What motivates the move? (character? revelation? time?)

**Step 5C: If camera is static, confirm the static is intentional**

Stillness is powerful. Don't move just to move.

Ask: Does this shot *need* to move?

Often the answer is: No. The character does.

### Output
- [ ] Camera movement decision made (move or static)
- [ ] If move: start, end, pace, motivation defined
- [ ] If static: intentionality confirmed
- [ ] Movement serves emotional and narrative goal

## Step 6: Direct the Actor (Nicodemus)

### Input
- Character's internal state (Step 3)
- Shot size and framing (Step 4)
- Camera movement (Step 5)
- Performance constraints from CHARACTER_BIBLE
- Previous shot's output state

### Process

**Step 6A: Determine emotional state**

What is Nicodemus feeling in this shot?

What produced that feeling?
- Previous action?
- Realization?
- Memory?
- Observation?
- Internal conflict?

**Step 6B: Determine emotional expression**

Emotion shows through:
- Eyes (gaze direction, pupil dilation, eye moisture, focus)
- Face (micro-expressions, tension, relaxation)
- Breath (visible, audible, paced)
- Hands (active, folded, trembling, still)
- Posture (open, closed, forward, withdrawn)
- Movement (measured, hesitant, fluid, arrested)

Ask: How does THIS character show THIS emotion?

Reference CHARACTER_BIBLE for Nicodemus's performance vocabulary.

**Step 6C: Determine specific blocking**

Where does Nicodemus stand/sit?
- Relative to camera?
- Relative to props?
- Relative to light?
- Relative to door/window/desk?

What does his body do?
- Enter/exit frame?
- Reach for object?
- Turn toward/away?
- Still or moving?

What do his hands do?
- Rest?
- Gesture?
- Hold object?
- Tremble?
- Clasp?

**Step 6D: Specify micro-direction**

Micro-expressions are powerful.

- Eyes: where do they focus? Do they widen or narrow?
- Brows: do they furrow? Raise?
- Mouth: tension or relaxation?
- Breath: visible inhale/exhale?
- Pauses: where does time stop?

**Step 6E: Confirm continuity with previous shot**

- Is his costume same state as previous shot output?
- Is his location consistent?
- Is his emotional arc continuous?
- Does his action lead from previous shot?

### Output
- [ ] Emotional state clearly stated
- [ ] How emotion shows (eyes, breath, hands, body) described
- [ ] Blocking in space defined
- [ ] Micro-direction specific and justified
- [ ] Continuity with previous shot confirmed

## Step 7: Specify Lighting

### Input
- Time of day (from Timeline)
- Location and available light sources (from Environment Bible)
- Emotional goal (Step 3)
- Character's location in frame

### Process

**Step 7A: Identify available light sources**

What light exists in this world?
- Window sunlight? (time of day determines angle)
- Moonlight? (if night)
- Candle? (position, intensity, flicker)
- Oil lamp? (position, color, intensity)
- Torch? (if outdoor)
- Reflected light? (from stone walls)

**Step 7B: Determine primary light direction**

Which source is dominant?
- Where does it come from? (angle, direction)
- How does it hit the subject?
- What shadows does it create?
- What mood does this direction create?

**Step 7C: Determine fill light**

Is there secondary light?
- From where?
- How much does it soften shadows?
- Does it serve emotional goal?

**Step 7D: Determine light quality**

How harsh or soft?
- Hard light = drama, clarity, definition
- Soft light = calm, gentleness, diffused
- Mixed = complex, realistic, naturalistic

**Step 7E: Determine color temperature**

What is the emotional color?
- Warm amber = candlelight, intimacy, calm, aging
- Cool blue = moonlight, questioning, uncertainty
- Golden = sunset, transition, nostalgia
- Balanced = naturalistic, honest, grounded

**Step 7F: Specify light changes if any**

Does light change during the shot?
- Candle flickers? (natural)
- Character moves into different light?
- Time appears to pass?

What CAUSES the change?
- Must be motivated by world (never unmotivated)

### Output
- [ ] Primary light source identified
- [ ] Light direction and angle specified
- [ ] Fill light determined
- [ ] Light quality stated (hard/soft)
- [ ] Color temperature described
- [ ] No unmotivated lighting added
- [ ] Light serves emotional goal

## Step 8: Create Shot Package

### Input
- All decisions from Steps 1-7
- Template from SHOT_PACKAGE_TEMPLATE.md

### Process

Fill the Shot Package template completely:
- Metadata (Shot ID, Scene ID, Gate, Status)
- Story (Narrative Purpose, Dramatic Beat, Emotional Goal, Director's Intention)
- Continuity (Input/Output state, Character, Prop, Environment, Light state)
- Camera (Size, Lens, Height, Distance, Movement, Composition, Framing, Focus, Blocking)
- Lighting (Time, Sources, Direction, Contrast, Color)
- Performance (Expression, Eyes, Hands, Body, Movement, Pace)
- Environment (Props, Background, Atmosphere, Dust, Smoke, Wind, Scroll/Table state)
- Image Generation (MASTER STYLE reference, Prompt placeholder, Negative placeholder)
- Video Generation (Camera motion, Character motion, Secondary motion, Physics, Timing)
- Quality Control (Checklists and validation)
- Deliverables (File format and naming)
- Revision History (first version logged)

### Output
- [ ] Shot Package complete in all 12 sections
- [ ] No creativity gaps or vague instructions
- [ ] Every decision justified
- [ ] All cross-references to Bibles included
- [ ] Continuity fully specified
- [ ] Timing matches MASTER_TIMELINE
- [ ] No contradictions with source material

### Input

- All source materials

### Process

**Checklist:**

- [ ] Does Shot Package connect to FILM_BLUEPRINT?
- [ ] Does it serve the Dramatic Route?
- [ ] Is narrative purpose clear?
- [ ] Is emotional goal achievable?
- [ ] Is camera motivation present?
- [ ] Is actor direction specific?
- [ ] Is lighting physically possible?
- [ ] Is continuity maintained?
- [ ] Does it fit timing requirement?
- [ ] Does it respect CHARACTER_BIBLE?
- [ ] Does it respect ENVIRONMENT_BIBLE?
- [ ] Is every decision documented?

### Output
- [ ] All validation checkpoints passed
- [ ] No ambiguities remain
- [ ] Shot Package is ready for approval

---

## Step 10: Approve or Revise

### Input
- Shot Package from Step 8
- Validation results from Step 9

### Decision

**If validation passes:**
- Mark Shot Package as APPROVED
- Record date and Director AI approval
- Transfer to Prompt Brain (next stage)

**If validation fails:**
- Identify specific gaps
- Revise Step(s) with issues
- Return to Step 8 (Create Shot Package)
- Revalidate

---

## Step 11: Transfer to Prompt Brain

### Input
- Approved Shot Package

### Output

Send to Prompt Brain:

```
SHOT PACKAGE: SHOT_001_A_ESTABLISHING
STATUS: APPROVED
DIRECTOR INTENT: [summary of emotional goal]
CREATIVE CONSTRAINTS: [critical rules]
REQUEST: Create generation prompt translating this Shot Package
DEADLINE: [as per production schedule]
```

Director AI does NOT create the prompt.
Director AI does NOT specify prompt syntax.
Director AI only sends the approved Shot Package.

Prompt Brain now owns the translation task.

---

## The Decision Engine is Not Intuition

It is a *system*.

Follow it completely. Do not skip steps.

Do not use "creative instinct" to bypass steps.

Every creative decision must trace back to:
- FILM_BLUEPRINT requirement, OR
- CHARACTER_BIBLE constraint, OR
- ENVIRONMENT_BIBLE specification, OR
- Shot Package purpose, OR
- Emotional goal

This is how great cinema is made: through *discipline*, not inspiration.
