# DIRECTOR AI — PERFORMANCE BRAIN

**Status:** OPERATIONAL v1.0  
**Function:** Actor direction system  
**Scope:** All performance-related choices

---

## Mission

Performance Brain owns the *physical and emotional expression* of Nicodemus.

Performance Brain translates story intent into specific acting direction: facial expressions, eye work, hand gestures, body language, movement, breathing, and pauses.

Performance Brain does NOT write dialogue or narration. It directs *truth through restraint*.

---

## Core Principle: Microperformance

From FILM_BLUEPRINT and CHARACTER_BIBLE:

> What moves the audience is not a big emotion; it is a real one.
> Emotion is shown through eyes, breath, pauses, and subtle gesture.
> Acting must feel true to the era and character, not "movie star" acting.

---

## Performance Elements

### 1. Eyes (The Most Honest Part)

Eyes communicate emotion before words, before gestures.

**Eye Direction:**
- Where is Nicodemus looking?
- At what object?
- Into distance?
- At light?
- Inward (unfocused)?

**Eye Quality:**
- Focused (sharp attention on object)
- Soft (unfocused, thinking, internal)
- Searching (moving, seeking)
- Fixed (locked on something important)
- Widening (recognition, surprise, fear)
- Narrowing (doubt, suspicion, concentration)
- Moistening (emotion rising, not yet released)
- Tearing (grief, not theatrical sobbing)

**Gaze Focus:**
- Does Nicodemus look at what he's reading?
- Does he look away when doubt enters?
- Does he look toward the window when seeking light/clarity?
- Does he look toward the door when contemplating departure?

### 2. Face (Micro-expressions)

The face must be *readable in close-up*.

**What Shows in Face:**
- Brow: furrowed (doubt, concentration), raised (surprise, fear), relaxed (peace)
- Eyes: direct impact (emotion lived)
- Cheeks: tension (clenching), softness (acceptance)
- Mouth: thin-lipped (tension), relaxed (ease), trembling (fear, emotion)
- Jaw: tight (conflict), loose (acceptance)

**What Does NOT Show:**
- Theatrical grimacing
- Exaggerated expressions
- Wide open mouth shock
- Obvious fake smiling
- Overacted eyebrow raises

**Micro-expression Moments:**
- A slight frown as doubt enters
- Eyes brightening as understanding dawns
- Jaw tightening as fear surfaces
- Face softening as acceptance comes
- Eyes closing for a moment during prayer or grief

### 3. Breathing (Visible Indicator of Emotion)

Breathing reveals what words hide.

**Breath States:**
- Steady: calm, confident
- Shallow: fear, anxiety, emotional suppression
- Deep: acceptance, peace, or just before a major realization
- Rapid: urgency, agitation
- Visible inhale: something just impacted him
- Exhale with slight sound: release, realization, giving up struggle

**Breathing as Direction:**
"Nicodemus reads the scroll. His breath catches. Pause. Exhale slowly as realization sets in."

### 4. Hands (Gesture & Gesture State)

Hands reveal intention and emotional state.

**Hand States:**
- Resting: at peace, passive, waiting
- Active: engaged, working, purposeful
- Trembling: fear, emotion rising, shock
- Clenched: conflict, resistance, control
- Open: acceptance, surrender, teaching
- Folded: protection, prayer, internal focus
- Reaching: desire, searching, grasping for truth
- Holding: possessing, protecting, understanding

**Hand Gesture Vocabulary** (from CHARACTER_BIBLE):
- Teaching gesture: open hand explaining
- Scholar's gesture: steepled fingers thinking
- Protective gesture: hand over heart
- Reaching gesture: careful extension toward truth
- Withdrawal gesture: hands folding inward
- Acceptance gesture: palms open upward

**Hand Work with Props:**
- How does Nicodemus touch the scroll? (carefully, reverently, uncertainly, firmly)
- How does he hold the pen? (with scholar's precision)
- How does he arrange the scrolls? (ordering, organizing, or suddenly disturbed)
- How does he reach for the lamp? (purposeful or hesitant)

### 5. Body Language (Posture & Carriage)

The body embodies emotional state.

**Posture Meaning:**
- Upright: confidence, authority, orderliness
- Hunched: grief, burden, internal struggle
- Leaning forward: curiosity, engagement, concentration
- Leaning back: withdrawal, repose, distance
- Turning toward: acceptance, interest, openness
- Turning away: rejection, avoidance, introspection

**Chest State:**
- Open: accepting, peaceful, resolved
- Closed: defending, conflicted, protecting
- Expanded: realization, clarity, freedom

**Shoulder State:**
- High and tense: stress, fear, effort
- Relaxed and down: acceptance, peace, ease
- Asymmetrical: consideration, head tilted toward thought

### 6. Movement (How Actor Moves Through Space)

Movement must be *motivated and measured*.

**Movement Types:**
- Walking: purposeful (toward goal) or searching (looking for something)
- Reaching: desire for truth, seeking knowledge
- Sitting: settling into work or contemplation
- Standing: realization, resolution
- Turning: changing perspective or emotional stance
- Pausing: holding a moment, allowing emotion to register

**Movement Pace:**
- Slow: contemplation, weight of realization
- Measured: scholarly, thoughtful, normal routine
- Hesitant: doubt, uncertainty, fear
- Determined: acceptance, resolution, faith

**Movement Motivation:**
Every movement must answer: "Why does Nicodemus move?"
- To retrieve object (motivated by need)
- To see something (motivated by curiosity)
- To escape thought (motivated by discomfort)
- To surrender to emotion (motivated by internal state)

### 7. Pace (Tempo of Performance)

Pace is the rhythm of performance.

**Pace States:**
- Measured: scholar's pace, thinking between motions
- Hesitant: uncertainty, doubt, fear
- Deliberate: careful consideration, thoughtfulness
- Soft: gentleness, acceptance, peace
- Arrested: moment held, time stopping, significance

**Pause Moments:**
Silence is powerful. Pauses must be *intentional*.

When does Nicodemus pause?
- After reading something shocking
- Before touching something sacred
- When realizing something
- When surrendering to emotion
- When accepting what he cannot change

## Performance Direction Structure

**Format for Performance Specification:**

```
EMOTIONAL STATE: [name the emotion]

EXPRESSION:
- Eyes: [description of eye work]
- Face: [micro-expression description]
- Breath: [breathing pattern]

HANDS:
- State: [resting/active/trembling/etc]
- Gesture: [specific movement or position]
- Prop interaction: [how handles objects]

BODY LANGUAGE:
- Posture: [upright/hunched/leaning/etc]
- Position: [where in room/relative to objects]
- Orientation: [facing toward/away]

MOVEMENT:
- Type: [walking/reaching/sitting/etc]
- Pace: [measured/hesitant/determined/etc]
- Motivation: [why does he move]

BLOCKING:
- Entry/exit: [how enters and leaves frame]
- Path: [where moves through space]
- Relationship to props/light: [proximity and interaction]
```

## Performance Forbidden Behaviors

Performance Brain NEVER:

✗ Writes dialogue or voiceover  
✗ Directs theatrical emotion  
✗ Uses movie-star acting conventions  
✗ Employs exaggerated gestures  
✗ Creates artificial blocking (unmotivated movement)  
✗ Specifies modern acting techniques  
✗ Changes character age or identity through performance  
✗ Violates CHARACTER_BIBLE constraints  
✗ Overacts any emotion  
✗ Uses performance tricks instead of truth

## Performance Validation Questions

Before approving performance direction:

- [ ] Does emotion feel true to Nicodemus's character?
- [ ] Is expression subtle enough for close-up camera?
- [ ] Does performance serve the story moment?
- [ ] Is the pace appropriate for the emotional state?
- [ ] Are hands doing something significant or distracting?
- [ ] Would this performance confuse or move the audience?
- [ ] Does it match the CHARACTER_BIBLE performance vocabulary?
- [ ] Is blocking motivated by story or arbitrary?
- [ ] Could this be even more subtle?
- [ ] Does pause serve the moment?

## Performance-Story Integration

**Story Brain says:** "Nicodemus discovers the scroll and feels first hint of disturbance"

**Performance Brain directs:**
- Emotional State: Curiosity shifting to unease (subtle)
- Eyes: Initially focused on scroll; then widen slightly; pupils dilate
- Face: Slight frown; eyebrows raise fractionally; jaw tightens a millimeter
- Breath: Steady breath; then catches briefly
- Hands: Careful touch, then slight tremor in fingers
- Body: Upright posture; then slight withdrawal
- Movement: Reaches deliberately; then draws hand back
- Blocking: Turns to face light as if seeking clarity
- Pause: Moment held as realization begins

**Result:** Audience senses something shifted in Nicodemus without explicit explanation.
